What to do after hail hits your home in Colorado Springs

By Greg Strauch, Farmers agent, Colorado license #963971 · Updated November 3, 2026

In the first 48 hours: photograph all the damage before anything is repaired, cover anything that's letting water in, file the claim, and look up your wind/hail deductible. Don't sign anything with a roofer yet. And never accept an offer to "cover your deductible." That's illegal in Colorado.

The 48-hour checklist

  1. Photograph everything before repairs. Take photos and video, with dates: the roof from the ground, siding, windows, gutters and downspouts, window screens, the fins on your AC unit, fences and your cars. Dented gutters and AC fins are easy evidence that hail was big enough to damage the roof.
  2. Stop further damage. Tarp the roof or cover broken windows, and keep every receipt. Your policy expects you to keep things from getting worse, and reasonable emergency costs are generally part of the claim.
  3. Report the claim. Call your insurer's claim line or your agent. Write down the claim number and the adjuster's name and phone number.
  4. Look up your wind/hail deductible. It's on your declarations page. On the Front Range it's often 1–2% of your dwelling coverage, not a flat $1,000. On a $500,000 house, that's $5,000–$10,000.
  5. Don't sign with a roofer yet. Don't sign a contract or hand over your claim to a contractor until you understand what you're signing and the adjuster has inspected.
  6. Refuse any "we'll cover your deductible" offer. Colorado law (C.R.S. 6-22-105) prohibits roofing contractors from paying, waiving or rebating your deductible.
  7. Get more than one estimate. Check the contractor's license with the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department at pprbd.org.

How the wind/hail deductible works, with real numbers

Say your house is insured for $450,000 of dwelling coverage with a 1% wind/hail deductible, and hail causes $22,000 of roof and gutter damage.

  • Your deductible is 1% of $450,000 = $4,500.
  • The claim pays $22,000 − $4,500 = $17,500, before any depreciation your policy holds back.
  • With a 2% deductible, you'd pay $9,000 and the claim would pay $13,000.

If the damage is less than the deductible, filing usually isn't worth it. Damage to your car is separate. It falls under your auto policy's comprehensive coverage and its own deductible.

Replacement cost vs. actual cash value on your roof

Some policies pay to replace a damaged roof at today's cost. Others pay actual cash value, which subtracts depreciation for the roof's age. On an older roof that difference can be thousands of dollars. Many replacement-cost policies pay in two checks: actual cash value first, then the held-back depreciation once the work is finished. Keep your final invoice.

Roofers who knock on your door after a storm

After every big hail day, out-of-town crews show up in Colorado Springs neighborhoods. Some are fine. The warning signs:

  • They offer to pay, waive or "work with" your deductible. That's illegal in Colorado.
  • They want you to sign before the adjuster comes out.
  • They ask you to sign over your insurance payments, or to let them "handle the claim."
  • They can't show a Pikes Peak Regional Building Department license.
  • They want a large payment up front.

How often this happens here

El Paso County had hail on 138 days from 2016 through 2025, the most of any county in Colorado, with golf-ball-size hail or larger on 46 of them. Most of it fell in the afternoon, May through August. See the full numbers in our 10-year El Paso County hail study.
