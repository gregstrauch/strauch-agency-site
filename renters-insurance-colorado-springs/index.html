What's different here

Three things about Colorado Springs your policy has to know.

Base and off-base housing

Privatized base housing at Fort Carson, Peterson and Schriever does not insure your belongings. Off-base, most Colorado Springs landlords now require proof of renters coverage at lease signing. Either way, you need your own policy.

Hail hits cars in parking lots

Renters insurance won't cover your car — that's your auto policy's comprehensive — but it does cover the TV, laptop and bike in the apartment when the roof leaks after the storm.

Liability follows you

The dog bites a neighbor at Palmer Park; a guest slips on your stairs. Personal liability on a renters policy covers it, and it can be bundled with auto for a discount that usually pays for the policy.

Our checklist

What we look at on every renters quote.

  • Personal property limit based on what it would cost to replace everything, not what you paid
  • Replacement cost, not actual cash value
  • Liability limit matched to your auto policy
  • Loss-of-use coverage for a hotel or short-term rental
  • Scheduled items: rings, firearms, instruments, cameras
  • Bundle with auto — the discount is often bigger than the renters premium

Questions we get

Straight answers.

How much does renters insurance cost in Colorado Springs?

Typically $12–25 a month for $30,000 of belongings and $100,000 of liability. Bundled with auto it's often close to free after the discount.

Does my landlord's insurance cover my stuff?

No. The landlord's policy covers the structure. Your belongings, your liability and your temporary housing are yours to insure.

Is renters insurance required in Colorado?

Not by law, but most leases require it, and privatized military housing strongly recommends it because the housing office's coverage doesn't extend to your property.

Does renters insurance cover my roommate?

Only if they're named on the policy. Each unrelated roommate needs their own policy or must be listed.
