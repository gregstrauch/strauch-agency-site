How to read your home insurance declarations page

By Greg Strauch, Farmers agent, Colorado license #921706 · Published November 12, 2026

Your declarations page is the one- or two-page summary at the front of your homeowners policy. It shows what is covered, for how much, and what you pay first when you file a claim. Here is how to read it in about ten minutes, with the parts that matter most in Colorado Springs.

What you need

Your current declarations page (often called the "dec page"). It comes with every new policy and every renewal. If you can't find it, your agent or your insurer's website can send a copy.

Step 1. Confirm the names and the address

The top of the page lists the named insured, the property address and the mortgage company. Everyone who owns the home should be named. If your mortgage was sold or refinanced, the lender listed here should be the current one, because claim checks for the structure are usually made out to both of you.

Step 2. Check the policy period

This is the start and end date of coverage, normally twelve months. Note the renewal date. The weeks before it are the easiest time to make changes or compare options.

Step 3. Read Coverage A, the dwelling limit

Coverage A is the most the policy pays to rebuild the house itself. It should match the cost to rebuild at today's labor and material prices, not the market value or your loan balance. Several other limits on the page are set as a percentage of this number, so if Coverage A is too low, they all are.

Step 4. Read Coverages B, C and D

Coverage B is other structures, such as a detached garage, shed or fence. Coverage C is personal property: furniture, clothes, electronics. Check whether it pays replacement cost or actual cash value, which subtracts depreciation. Coverage D is loss of use, which pays for a place to live while your home is repaired.

Step 5. Read Coverages E and F, your liability

Coverage E is personal liability, for when someone is hurt on your property or you damage someone else's. Coverage F is medical payments to others, a small no-fault amount for a guest's injury. Compare Coverage E with the liability limit on your auto policy. The two should match.

Step 6. Find every deductible, in dollars

Most Colorado policies show two: an all-other-perils deductible and a separate wind and hail deductible. The wind and hail deductible is often a percentage of Coverage A. One percent of a $500,000 dwelling limit is $5,000. Write the dollar figure next to the percentage.

Step 7. Go through the endorsements list

Endorsements are the add-ons and changes to the standard policy, listed by form name or number. This is where you find out whether you have water backup coverage, scheduled valuables, extended replacement cost or a roof payment schedule. If a form name means nothing to you, ask your agent what it does.

Step 8. Check the premium and the discounts

The page shows the annual premium and usually the discounts applied. Look for ones you should have and don't: multi-policy, a newer or impact-resistant roof, a monitored alarm. Tell your agent if anything has changed.

What the declarations page won't tell you

It is a summary. Exclusions, such as flood and earth movement, and special limits on jewelry, firearms and cash are in the policy itself. If your page lists no flood policy and no water backup endorsement, assume you don't have those coverages.
