Hail in El Paso County, 2016–2025: what 10 years of NOAA records show

By Greg Strauch, Farmers agent, Colorado license #963971 · Published November 3, 2026

The short version: From 2016 through 2025, El Paso County had more hail reports (756) and more days with hail (138) than any other county in Colorado. It also had the most reports of hail 2 inches or larger. Hail fell on about 14 days a year on average. Nearly all of it fell between May and August, and three out of four reports came in the afternoon.

How El Paso County compares to the rest of Colorado

NOAA's Storm Events Database recorded 4,491 hail reports in Colorado's counties from 2016 through 2025. El Paso County accounts for 756 of them, about 17% of the statewide total and far ahead of Weld County in second place.

The quietest year (2022) still had 5 hail days and hail 2 inches across. That's the takeaway for homeowners: a "light" hail year here can still produce roof-damaging hail.

When hail falls

By month. Of the 138 hail days, 41 were in July, 35 in June, 30 in August and 28 in May. Only 4 fell outside May through August. The earliest was March 29 (2019) and the latest was September 14 (2021).

By time of day. 74% of reports came between 1 p.m. and 7 p.m. Mountain Daylight Time. The single busiest hour was 4 to 5 p.m. Overnight hail is rare but not unheard of. The June 13, 2018 storm hit after midnight with hail up to 3 inches.

The 8 biggest hail days, 2016–2025

Ranked by the number of separate hail reports NOAA logged in the county that day.

  1. July 28, 2016: 42 reports, up to 2.5″ (tennis ball). Heaviest around Peterson, Falcon and Black Forest.
  2. August 5, 2023: 36 reports, up to 4″ (softball) at Schriever, with 3.25″ in Fountain.
  3. August 6, 2018: 30 reports, up to 4″ (softball) in Widefield. NOAA cites media reports of about $172 million in damage to vehicles, roofs, siding and windows, and eight people were taken to the hospital from the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo.
  4. July 7, 2016: 21 reports, up to 1.75″ (golf ball), concentrated around Falcon.
  5. June 15, 2025: 21 reports, up to 1.75″, across Peterson, Colorado Springs and Falcon.
  6. July 8, 2016: 20 reports, up to 2.5″, Black Forest, the Air Force Academy and Monument.
  7. July 31, 2023: 20 reports, up to 1.5″, mostly within Colorado Springs.
  8. July 6, 2025: 20 reports, up to 2″ (hen egg), Colorado Springs, Black Forest, Falcon and Manitou Springs.

One more worth knowing: June 13, 2018 (overnight, up to 3″) is listed by NOAA at about $169 million in damage, based on media reports. Two storms eight weeks apart that summer did a combined $340 million or so.

Where it was reported

The most-reported locations were Colorado Springs (107 reports), Peterson Space Force Base (99), Black Forest (94), Falcon (80), the Air Force Academy (46), Manitou Springs (40), Monument (39) and Fountain (35). Military installations show up high partly because they have trained weather observers, so don't read this as a ranking of neighborhoods. Hail hit every part of the county.

What this means for your insurance

  • Know your wind/hail deductible before May. Many Front Range policies carry a separate one, often 1–2% of dwelling coverage. On a $500,000 house that's $5,000–$10,000.
  • Check how your roof is valued. Some policies pay replacement cost on a roof; others pay actual cash value, which subtracts depreciation for an older roof. The difference on a hail claim can be thousands of dollars.
  • Don't drop comprehensive on your car if it's parked outside. Hail damage to vehicles is covered under comprehensive, not collision.
  • Keep the checklist handy. Our guide to what to do after a hailstorm covers photos, temporary repairs, the claim, and Colorado's law against roofers covering your deductible.

Method and data

Source: NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information, Storm Events Database, yearly detail files for 2016–2025, downloaded November 3, 2026. We included every record with event type "Hail" and state "Colorado," and counted county-level records only.

Definitions: A "report" is one NOAA hail record. A "hail day" is a calendar date with at least one report in the county. Sizes are the reported diameter. NOAA's standard comparisons: 1″ quarter, 1.75″ golf ball, 2″ hen egg, 2.5″ tennis ball, 2.75″ baseball, 4″ softball. NOAA lists times in Mountain Standard Time; we converted them to daylight time for the summer months.

Limits: The database records hail that someone reported, mostly trained spotters (378 of the 756 reports) and the public (260). Hail under 1 inch is often not logged, and rural hail is undercounted. NOAA has dollar damage estimates for only three of these 756 records, so we haven't tried to total damage. 2026 is excluded because NOAA's 2026 records are still preliminary.
