7 things to check before you choose an insurance agent in Colorado Springs

By Greg Strauch, Farmers agent, Colorado license #921706 · Published November 12, 2026

Colorado Springs has hundreds of licensed insurance agents, and price alone won't tell you which one to pick. These seven checks take about fifteen minutes and work for any agent, including us.

1. Confirm the license with the state

Anyone selling insurance in Colorado must hold a producer license from the Colorado Division of Insurance. The Division has a free license lookup on its website where you can search by name or license number and see whether the license is active. It takes two minutes and almost nobody does it.

Check: search the agent's name on the Colorado Division of Insurance license lookup. Ours is #921706.

2. Know whether the agent is captive or independent

A captive agent represents one insurance company. An independent agent can quote several. Neither is better for everyone. A captive agent tends to know one company's coverages, discounts and claims process in depth. An independent agent can shop more companies for you. We are a Farmers agency, so we quote Farmers. If you want several companies compared side by side, an independent agent is the right call, and a good agent of either kind will tell you that.

Check: ask, "Which companies can you quote for me?"

3. Find out who answers after the sale

The quote is the easy part. What matters is who picks up when you have a claim, a billing problem or a new teen driver. Some offices hand you to a call center once the policy is sold. Others keep service in the local office.

Check: ask, "If I have a claim, do I call you or an 800 number? Who is my contact?"

4. Test their Colorado Springs knowledge

Insurance here is not like insurance in most of the country. El Paso County had more hail reports than any other Colorado county from 2016 through 2025 (our hail study), wildfire risk affects which homes can be insured on the west side and in Black Forest, and thousands of military families move in and out every year. A local agent should be able to talk about all three without looking anything up.

Check: ask, "What is the wind and hail deductible on this quote, in dollars?" A clear answer is a good sign.

5. See if they review coverage or only price

A lower premium often means lower limits or a higher deductible, not a better deal. A good agent walks you through what they recommend, why, and what it costs to add or drop each piece, so you choose with the tradeoffs in front of you.

Check: ask for a side-by-side of your current policy and the new quote, limit by limit. These seven home policy gaps are a good test.

6. Read recent reviews, and the replies

Look at Google reviews and at least one other source. Recent reviews matter more than the total count. Read how the agency replies to complaints. Reviews that mention a claim tell you more than reviews about a fast quote.

Check: sort reviews by newest and look for claim stories.

7. Ask how renewals are handled

Rates and home values change every year. An agent who reviews your policy at renewal catches a dwelling limit that has fallen behind rebuild costs or a discount you now qualify for. An agent who only calls when you threaten to leave does not.

Check: ask, "Will someone review my policy with me every year?" Our renewal checklist shows what that review should cover.

Military family?

Add one more. Ask whether the agent has handled a PCS move, a deployment and a vehicle in storage. Coverage needs change at each step, and an agent near Fort Carson, Peterson and Schriever should know them. Our PCS coverage timeline lays out what to do and when.

Five questions for the first call

  • Which companies can you quote?
  • What is my wind and hail deductible in dollars?
  • Is my roof covered at replacement cost or actual cash value?
  • Who do I call when I have a claim?
  • Will you review my policy with me every year?
